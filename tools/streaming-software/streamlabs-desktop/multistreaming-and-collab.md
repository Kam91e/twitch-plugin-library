# Streamlabs Desktop — Multi-Streaming & Collab Cam

> Broadcast to Twitch, YouTube, TikTok, and Facebook simultaneously, output horizontal and vertical layouts at the same time, and bring guests onto your stream with a shareable link — all part of the Streamlabs Ultra package.

## Overview

Streamlabs Desktop bundles three collaboration-focused capabilities under its Ultra subscription ($27/mo or $179/yr): **multi-streaming**, **Dual Output**, and **Collab Cam**. Together they let a single streamer reach multiple audiences at once — desktop viewers on Twitch, mobile viewers on TikTok or YouTube Shorts, and remote guests joining from any browser — without leaving the Streamlabs Desktop interface.

- **Multi-streaming** broadcasts your stream to several platforms at the same time, including Twitch, YouTube, TikTok, and Facebook.
- **Dual Output** renders a horizontal and a vertical layout from the same broadcast, so mobile-first platforms get a native vertical feed.
- **Collab Cam** turns a shareable link into a guest appearance, making interviews, co-op play, and IRL segments simple to set up.

All three features require Streamlabs Ultra; they are part of the same subscription as the other premium Streamlabs features (expanded Cloudbot, all overlay themes, the Desktop App Store, and reduced tipping fees).

## Configuration

### Multi-Streaming (Ultra)

1. Sign in to your Streamlabs account and go to the Streamlabs dashboard at streamlabs.com
2. Connect the accounts you want to broadcast to — Twitch, YouTube, TikTok, Facebook, and other supported platforms
3. In Streamlabs Desktop, open the **Go Live** window and select the destinations you want to send your stream to
4. Configure per-destination settings such as title, category, and stream key before going live

Each connected platform broadcasts your same video and audio feed, so setup time is limited to authorising each account and choosing your destinations per stream.

### Dual Output (Ultra)

Dual Output lets you stream a **horizontal** and a **vertical** layout at the same time. The vertical output targets mobile platforms such as TikTok and YouTube Shorts, while the horizontal output continues to serve Twitch, YouTube, and desktop viewers.

- Enable Dual Output in the Streamlabs Desktop output settings
- Assign your existing sources to the vertical scene layout — you can choose which webcam, game capture, and overlays appear in each orientation
- Go live as usual; both outputs are broadcast from the single Streamlabs Desktop instance
- Note that each additional platform you broadcast to consumes additional upload bandwidth, so factor your connection speed into how many destinations you enable

### Collab Cam (Ultra)

Collab Cam brings guests onto your stream through a simple shareable link:

1. Open Collab Cam in Streamlabs Desktop and generate a link
2. Send the link to your guest — they join in their browser, no app install required
3. The guest appears as a video source in your scene, ready to be positioned like any other source
4. Invite up to 11 guests at once — enough for interviews, panel discussions, or co-op games

Because guests join through a browser, they can appear on stream from almost any device, which makes Collab Cam useful for IRL segments where the guest is on a phone.

## Tips

- Test your multi-stream destinations on a private stream before your first public multi-platform broadcast — each platform has its own moderation and chat rules
- Use Dual Output's vertical feed for TikTok and YouTube Shorts while keeping your main horizontal layout for Twitch — the same stream reaches both audiences
- Set up your vertical scene layout once and reuse it every stream; source positions in the vertical layout are independent of the horizontal one
- Collab Cam is ideal for interviews and co-op games — share the link a few minutes early so guests can join and check their audio before you go live
- Keep an eye on your upload speed: every simultaneous destination (multi-stream and Dual Output) adds bandwidth usage, which can affect stream quality on slower connections
- If you only ever stream to one platform in one orientation, multi-streaming and Dual Output may not be worth the Ultra cost — weigh them against the other Ultra perks
