# Get to Know Twitch

> This path introduces the foundational concepts every new Twitch streamer needs: how to behave on the platform, how to build a safe and well-moderated community, and what rules and policies govern your channel. Understanding these basics early helps you avoid common pitfalls and create a positive environment from day one. It covers practical etiquette guidelines, assembling moderators and support roles, and navigating Twitch's Terms of Service and Community Guidelines. Whether you're brand new or refining your approach, these three chapters set the groundwork for sustainable growth on Twitch.

## Details

### Twitch Etiquette

Every broadcast is a representation of you, your brand, and your community. Modeling the behavior you want viewers to emulate helps curate a community that reflects your values. Establishing clear rules and boundaries for your chat is essential — Twitch lets you set a custom message that new chatters must agree to before participating. Key practices include connecting with other creators, celebrating your viewers and raid communities, and avoiding self-promotion in other streamers' channels. Many streamers keep their viewer count hidden, and drawing attention to another streamer's numbers is generally considered poor etiquette.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/get-to-know-twitch/twitch-etiquette/>

### Assembling a Support Squad

Building a safe and thriving community on Twitch relies heavily on having the right people in your corner. Moderators are your channel's backbone — they greet viewers, enforce rules, shape conversations, and can initiate Shield Mode, timeouts, or bans as needed. Beyond moderators, Twitch offers additional roles like Editors (who can manage dashboard content and run ads), VIPs (who receive special badges and bypass slow mode), and Artists (community members who contribute to your channel's visual identity). Tools like AutoMod can hold potentially inappropriate messages for review, and the Twitch Safety Center provides comprehensive resources for channel protection. Well-moderated chats make new viewers feel welcome, while poorly moderated ones can drive people away permanently.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/get-to-know-twitch/assembling-a-support-squad/>

### Policies, Guidelines and Terms

Understanding Twitch's rule structure is essential for maintaining a positive channel experience. The platform is governed by three key documents: Terms of Service (rules for all users), Community Guidelines (standards for acceptable behavior), and Terms of Sales (rules governing purchases). Violations lead to enforcement actions ranging from warnings to temporary or indefinite suspensions, with an appeals process available at appeals.twitch.tv for those who believe an enforcement was issued in error. Viewers can report violations confidentially through each streamer's channel page, though mass reporting does not speed up investigation. Successful appeals typically include remorse, accountability, or additional context demonstrating an error was made.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/get-to-know-twitch/policies-guidelines-and-terms/>
