# Going Live

> This path walks you through the practical steps of actually broadcasting on Twitch, whether you're on a PC, mobile device, or console. It covers the platform-specific setup for each device type — including PC via OBS, mobile IRL and gaming streams, PlayStation, and Xbox — so you can start streaming from wherever you are. Beyond the physical setup, you'll learn how to use the Creator Dashboard as your central management hub, the Stream Manager for real-time broadcast control, and the technical specifications for optimizing video and audio quality. The path also helps you identify what equipment and knowledge you actually need to get started, without overcomplicating your setup.

## Details

### Stream on PC

Starting a stream from your PC is straightforward: log in or create a Twitch account, set up broadcasting software (such as OBS) and a capture card if needed, add a title/category/tags in your Creator Dashboard, and go live. To improve stream quality over time, upgrading your audio equipment and camera is recommended — audio quality is especially critical for connecting with viewers. The chapter also points to the Establishing Your Brand path for deeper guidance on creating engaging and interactive streams.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/going-live/stream-on-pc/>

### Stream on Mobile (IRL)

You can create content on Twitch from anywhere using just your phone. The setup process is simple: download the Twitch app, log in or create an account, tap the plus button, grant audio and camera permissions, update your stream information, and tap Stream to go live. This makes IRL (in real life) streaming one of the most accessible entry points for new creators.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/going-live/stream-on-mobile-irl/>

### Stream Mobile Gaming

Mobile gaming streams follow nearly the same steps as IRL mobile streaming, with the added step of selecting which game you want to broadcast. Download the Twitch app, log in, tap the plus button, grant permissions, choose your game from the list, update your stream information, and go live. This chapter makes it clear that broadcasting mobile gameplay requires no special hardware beyond your phone.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/going-live/stream-mobile-gaming/>

### Stream on PlayStation

PlayStation has built-in Twitch streaming support. To go live, select a game, navigate to the Broadcast section, log in or create a Twitch account (from a mobile device or computer), update your stream information, and start broadcasting. For improved quality, you can upgrade to a PlayStation Camera for facecam and use a capture card with a streaming PC for more engaging elements. The chapter emphasizes that there is no pressure to plan the perfect stream — your path as a creator is unique whether streaming as a hobby or full-time.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/going-live/stream-on-playstation/>

### Stream on Xbox

Xbox streaming is equally built-in: navigate to Capture & Share, log in or create a Twitch account, select a game, update your stream information, and go live. As with PlayStation, you can enhance your broadcasts with a camera, capture card, or streaming PC for more interactive elements. The chapter encourages new streamers to get started with what they have — no stream is perfect, and starting where you are is completely acceptable.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/going-live/stream-on-xbox/>

### Creator Dashboard

The Creator Dashboard is the central hub for managing all aspects of your Twitch channel. It provides access to ten key areas: Content (past broadcasts, clips, highlights), Monetization (subscriptions, Bits, ads), Moderation (banned users, AutoMod), Settings (channel preferences, branding, schedule), Viewer Rewards (channel points, emotes, Drops), Stream Together (cross-channel collaboration), Streaming Tools (third-party connections and broadcasting apps), Charity (fundraisers and donations), Extensions (interactive channel applications), and the Safety Center (rules and reporting). Each section is designed to help you track growth, manage revenue, and engage with your community effectively.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/going-live/creator-dashboard/>

### Stream Manager

The Stream Manager is your real-time command center, providing activity feeds, chat, and broadcast tools all in one place. Key features include the ability to edit your stream title (aim for goal-oriented and exciting), set custom go-live notifications (as conversation starters or mission announcements), choose your broadcast category (which can be changed mid-stream), and research relevant tags to help viewers discover you. The Stream Manager layout is fully customizable with panels like Quick Actions (for raids, clips, polls), Activity Feed (Bits, subs, interactions), and Collaboration (tracking friends' live status). Understanding how to configure these elements before going live significantly improves your broadcast's discoverability and professionalism.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/going-live/stream-manager/>

### What You Need to Stream

The technical side of streaming involves balancing several key variables: bitrate (data transmitted per second affecting quality), encoding (converting raw video/audio to compressed formats), video quality (determined by resolution and frame rate), and audio quality (compression, channels, and data rate). Twitch provides official broadcast guidelines to help find the right balance, and the Twitch Inspector tool can diagnose stream health issues or run test broadcasts. The chapter stresses not to overcomplicate your tech setup before you're ready — plan upgrades incrementally and share your growth goals with your community. Researching relevant tags and experimenting with your Quick Actions panel are also recommended next steps.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/going-live/what-you-need-to-stream/>
