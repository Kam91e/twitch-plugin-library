# Content Categories on Twitch

> This path is a comprehensive tour of Twitch's non-gaming and gaming content categories, helping you understand where your stream fits and how to make the most of each category. It covers gaming strategies (game selection, genre pathways, and community interaction tools), real-world IRL streaming (setup, safety, and engagement), Just Chatting and ASMR (conversation techniques and relaxation content), Creative and Coding streams (camera setup, show-and-tell, and co-working), Co-Streaming and Sidecasting (watching licensed events together), Fitness and Health, Food and Drink, Music and DJs (including the Twitch DJ Program), and VTubing (digital avatar creation and streaming). Each chapter provides category-specific setup advice, streamer strategies, and interaction tools to help you build a community around your chosen content.

## Details

### Gaming

Gaming accounts for approximately 80% of all content on Twitch, spanning casual to competitive to esports. Choosing the right games means balancing your personal interests with viewer demand and current trends, then selecting a pathway — singular game (deep expertise in one title), singular genre (one game type with flexibility), or variety (broad content across all genres). Twitch offers powerful interaction tools like Channel Points, Polls, Stream Together, Drops, and Extensions to keep your gaming community engaged and coming back for more.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/content-categories-on-twitch/gaming/>

### IRL

IRL streams take your audience beyond gaming into the real world — sightseeing, fishing, exploring hidden gems, and more. The basic setup requires only a phone and a stable internet connection, though you can enhance the experience with wireless microphones, tripods, alert speakers, and gimbal stabilizers. Safety is paramount: always be aware of your surroundings, avoid showing personal information like license plates or addresses, know local laws about filming in public, and get permission before streaming in private venues.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/content-categories-on-twitch/irl/>

### Just Chatting, ASMR, & Talk Shows

Just Chatting is Twitch's most popular category, focusing on direct audience interaction through conversation, storytelling, and personal connection. ASMR streams use gentle sounds and ambiance to create a tranquil, relaxing experience, while Talk Shows bring hosts and guests together for structured discussions on topics ranging from gaming news to pop culture. Success in all three sub-categories depends on your ability to maintain engaging conversation, manage energy levels, and use interactive tools like Channel Points, Polls, and Community Challenges to keep viewers participating.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/content-categories-on-twitch/just-chatting-asmr-talk-shows/>

### Creative, Coding, & Co-working

Creative streams showcase the journey of making art — from digital illustrations and cosplay to 3D printing and needlework — requiring thoughtful camera placement, proper lighting, and audio quality. The Software and Game Development category lets viewers watch coding in real-time, offering feedback and learning alongside the streamer. Co-working streams leverage body doubling and productivity techniques like the Pomodoro method to create a quiet, focused environment where streamers and viewers work productively side by side, using break times for community interaction.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/content-categories-on-twitch/creative-coding-co-working/>

### Co-Streams & Sidecasting

Co-streaming lets you add your facecam and commentary on top of licensed Twitch content — such as conventions, events, and award shows — creating a personalized viewing experience alongside your community. Sidecasting focuses entirely on your real-time reactions and analysis without displaying the original content's visuals or audio, making it suitable when co-streaming isn't permitted. Both formats require careful attention to copyright: research what's approved, avoid saving or redistributing co-streamed content, and structure your broadcast to keep viewers engaged even when the main event is paused.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/content-categories-on-twitch/co-streams-and-sidecasting/>

### Fitness & Health

The Fitness and Health category welcomes streamers at every stage of their wellness journey, from beginners starting a fitness routine to competitive bodybuilders. Proper setup involves multiple camera angles to show form and movements, well-placed audio to ensure you're heard during workouts, and themed content schedules like "Mobility Mondays" to build viewer habits. Engaging your community through Q&A segments, workout challenges, and Channel Point redemptions that influence your exercises creates a supportive, interactive environment that motivates both you and your viewers.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/content-categories-on-twitch/fitness-and-health/>

### Food & Drink

Food and Drink streams invite viewers into your kitchen for live cooking experiences — from social eating and snack reviews to complex recipe challenges. A two-camera setup captures both your face and close-up prep work, while proper lighting and a clear microphone ensure viewers can see ingredients and hear your commentary over the sizzle. Planning your stream around cooking times, mixing personal favorites with trending food from social media, and incorporating interactive elements like cook-alongs and community challenges keep viewers engaged from start to finish.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/content-categories-on-twitch/food-and-drink/>

### Music & DJs

Whether you're a producer, singer-songwriter, or instrumental performer, Twitch provides a platform to share your talent with a global audience. Audio quality is the top priority — optimal streaming software settings, balanced mixer levels, and clear sound output ensure your music shines. The Twitch DJ Program allows DJs to livestream millions of tracks without risk of takedowns, though VODs, Clips, and Highlights cannot be saved when opted in. Both the Music Club and DJ Club Creator Clubs offer communities for networking, sharing setup tips, and finding collaboration opportunities.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/content-categories-on-twitch/music-and-djs/>

### VTubing

VTubing combines traditional streaming with digital avatars — ranging from simple PNGTubers (2D images activated by voice) to Live2D models with real-time face tracking to full 3D avatars using motion capture technology. Developing a pre-stream checklist to test your model's tracking, audio levels, and scene layouts ensures a smooth broadcast. VTubers have unique engagement opportunities like triggering toggles or background changes with Channel Point redemptions, creating dynamic scenes that invite viewers to actively participate in the show.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/content-categories-on-twitch/vtubing/>
