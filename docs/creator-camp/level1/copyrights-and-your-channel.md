# Copyrights and Your Channel

> This path is Twitch's official Copyright School and legal reference for streamers, designed to help you understand and comply with copyright law while creating content. It covers the fundamentals of what copyright is, why it matters for streamers, what happens when you receive a DMCA notification, the resources and tools Twitch provides, a quiz to test your knowledge, a detailed glossary of copyright and DMCA terms, and practical habits for building a legally sound streaming practice. Completing Copyright School and passing the quiz can remove one copyright strike from your channel, making it both an educational and restorative resource.

## Details

### Copyright School: What do I need to know about copyright law?

Copyright School is Twitch's introductory educational resource that covers the fundamentals of copyright law, the DMCA (Digital Millennium Copyright Act), and how they apply to streaming on Twitch. A copyright gives the owner of an original work — including music, artwork, novels, TV shows, movies, video games, and code — the exclusive right to control how that work is used, and using it without permission may constitute infringement. Eligible streamers who complete Copyright School and pass the associated quiz can have one copyright strike removed from their channel, with a 28-day completion window and 12-month eligibility refresh.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/copyrights-and-your-channel/what-is-a-copyright-and-what-do-i-need-to-know-about-copyright-law/>

### Copyright School: Why are copyright laws important for me to think about?

As a streamer, you are legally responsible for everything you broadcast and upload to your channel, including ensuring that material doesn't infringe someone else's copyright. Twitch's Terms of Service and DMCA Guidelines outline what content is not permitted — such as restreaming copyrighted works without permission, playing unauthorized music, or streaming embargoed video games — versus what is permitted, including original content and content you've been properly licensed or authorized to stream. A good rule of thumb: if you own the rights or have explicit authorization, you should know it; if you're unsure, you most likely don't have the rights.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/copyrights-and-your-channel/why-are-copyright-laws-important-for-me-to-think-about-when-i-stream/>

### Copyright School: What happens if you violate copyright law?

When Twitch receives a valid DMCA notification identifying infringing content on your channel, it is legally obligated to remove that content and issue a copyright strike under its three-strikes-and-you're-out repeat infringer policy. Streamers can dispute DMCA notifications by contacting the claimant to seek a retraction or by submitting a counter-notification — though the latter can have real legal consequences and should only be used when you are certain no infringement occurred. Fair use and fair dealing are legal defenses that may apply in limited circumstances (commentary, criticism, education, parody), but they are fact-dependent, would need to be raised in court, and do not prevent you from being sued or having content taken down.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/copyrights-and-your-channel/what-happens-if-you-violate-copyright-law/>

### Copyright School: Resources & Tools

Twitch provides several resources and tools to help streamers stay informed about copyright issues and build good streaming habits. Key tools include Copyright Audio Warnings (which notify you when multiple instances of copyrighted audio are detected in your VODs and may toggle your auto-publish setting off), multi-track audio support in Twitch Studio and OBS (allowing different audio for live streams versus archives), the Copyright Claims Manager (for reviewing DMCA notifications), and a curated list of recommended third-party licensed music services. The Twitch DMCA Guidelines, Music Guidelines, and Terms of Service are the primary reference documents for understanding your obligations.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/copyrights-and-your-channel/copyright-school-resources-tools/>

### Copyright School: Quiz

The Copyright School quiz tests your understanding of copyright law and DMCA concepts covered in the previous chapters. Topics include identifying what constitutes a copyrighted work, who submits DMCA notifications, the international applicability of the DMCA, proper uses of copyrighted material, the limitations of fair use, and the difference between a VOD muting and a copyright strike. Successfully completing the quiz — along with the preceding Copyright School chapters — will result in one copyright strike being removed from your channel.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/copyrights-and-your-channel/copyright-school-quiz/>

### Understanding Copyright Law and the DMCA as a Twitch Streamer

This chapter serves as a comprehensive glossary of key copyright and DMCA terms that streamers may need to reference regularly. It defines copyright as intellectual property giving owners exclusive rights over their original works, explains how copyright infringement occurs when works are used without permission, describes the DMCA notification and takedown process, and covers counter-notifications, retractions, Twitch's repeat infringer policy, and the nuances of fair use and fair dealing. The chapter emphasizes that while fair use can protect certain uses (criticism, commentary, education, parody), it is a legal defense that must be raised in court and does not prevent initial takedowns.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/copyrights-and-your-channel/understanding-copyright-law-and-the-dmca-as-a-twitch-streamer/>

### Resources and Tools to Help Streamers Build Good Habits

Building good streaming habits around copyright starts with proactive choices: use licensed third-party music (or copyright-free tracks and custom compositions), create original visuals and overlays, and regularly review your content through Twitch's Video Producer and Copyright Claims Manager. Twitch's audio detection system scans VODs and Clips for copyrighted audio, muting portions and deleting Clips where such audio is found — if three or more instances are detected in 24 hours, your VOD auto-publish setting is toggled off and you're notified. Stream Settings in the Creator Dashboard let you control VOD storage, publication defaults, and category-specific publishing, while multi-track audio in Twitch Studio and OBS allows different audio for live streams versus archives.

**Source:** <https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/copyrights-and-your-channel/resources-and-tools-to-help-streamers-build-good-habits/>
